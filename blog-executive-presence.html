Executive Presence: A Cornerstone for Women Leaders

June 26, 2025

Executive presence (EP) is often defined as the ability to inspire confidence — projecting credibility, composure, and clarity so others believe you can lead under pressure. Sylvia Ann Hewlett’s research highlights three key pillars: gravitas, communication, and appearance — a framework reaffirmed in studies spanning 2012 to 2022. Now, leadership requires inclusiveness – creating a space where everyone’s voice matters. Face-to-face charisma is no longer enough – you need to make your presence felt through the screen through mastering the art of virtual communication.

Yet for women leaders, navigating executive presence comes with unique challenges. Due to role-congruity bias, women who display traits like assertiveness or decisiveness can face backlash or be labeled “bossy”. This ‘double-bind’ requires women to balance authority and warmth — a balance men rarely need to strike.

1. Gravitas with Authenticity

Gravitas isn’t about a stern demeanor — it’s about calm confidence, intentionality, and conveying that critical “under-control” aura. Research shows women should amplify gravitas in ways aligned with their style — thoughtful pauses, poised posture, moderated tone — projecting presence without triggering bias.

2. Communication That Connects

Clear, structured delivery builds trust — and so does cultivating warmth. Amy Cuddy’s work on competence and warmth suggests leaders combine both to drive influence. Present results-focused narratives, then reinforce them with empathy — e.g., “This is what we achieved — and here’s how we’ll support each other moving forward.”

3. Strategic Visibility & Self-Promotion

Women often under-promote achievements due to fear of backlash — alongside stereotype threats. One study noted women were 28% less likely than men to self-promote on platforms like X. To build executive presence, women need to strategically share wins, advocate for themselves, and highlight impact. Frame accomplishments in service to organizational goals to sidestep negative perceptions.

4. Build Authentic Authority

Traditional EP norms often prioritized a stiff managerial posture. Now, inclusive research is reshaping definitions to value emotional intelligence, collaboration, and being “fully you.” Lean into what makes your leadership unique — empathy, active listening, cultural awareness. Companies benefit when women bring diverse executive presence rather than mimicking male archetypes.

5. Advocate for Structural Support

The ‘glass cliff’ phenomenon — appointing women during crises — underlines how EP alone doesn’t level the playing field. Without sponsorship, resources, or mentoring, women risk high-stakes failure. C-suite sponsors must invest in developing women’s EP over time — not just in emergency conditions.

Building Your Executive Presence: A Practical Action Plan

  • Gravitas — adopt intentional posture; practice pausing before responding; maintain a steady tone under stress.
  • Communication — tell result-driven stories; weave in emotional connection; adjust delivery for clarity.
  • Visibility — share successes in team meetings and updates; mentor peers; volunteer for visible initiatives.
  • Authenticity — reflect on personal style and values; seek feedback on how presence lands; refine your “on-stage” persona.
  • Support systems — build a circle of champions; seek sponsors; ask for stretch opportunities before crises arise.

Bring It All Together

Executive presence is not a mask, but a craft – a blend of credible gravitas, connection-building communication, and authentic self-expression. For women, it’s about mastering the balance: showing up confidently and genuinely, asserting influence and inclusion. As EP norms evolve, women who claim their natural authority – and build supportive structures – will shape the future of leadership.
