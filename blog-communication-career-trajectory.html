How Communication Shapes the Career Trajectory of Women in Senior Leadership

July 10, 2025

After decades working with Fortune 500 companies, I’ve seen one factor stand out: how women communicate at senior levels profoundly influences not only perceptions of their leadership, but also their promotion opportunities and longevity in powerful roles. Below, we explore why this is true — and how women can strengthen their impact through strategic communication.

1. Communication as a Career Catalyst

Research shows women who reach the C-suite tend to leverage transformational communication — emotionally intelligent, relationship-focused, and inclusive messaging that both inspires and guides teams. For instance, a recent Indonesian study found women’s transformational leadership significantly boosted employee engagement partly through their communication style.

However, gender norms and stereotypes often silence women’s voices. A qualitative study highlighted that gender-based communication barriers — like being perceived as too soft or too aggressive — can hold women back. Often, women are caught in a double bind: using assertive language gains competence, but can reduce likability; staying relational risks being labeled “ineffectual.” Another study by KPMG delves into why women aspire to lead but are hesitant to shake the lessons learned growing up that can hold them back.

2. Visibility Through Vocal Confidence

Senior roles require visibility — and that means speaking up. One study on academic seminars showed women ask far fewer questions than men, reducing their visibility and undermining perceptions of leadership presence. In business, the stakes are even higher: speaking up in meetings, presenting ideas, and asking for resources all depend on vocal confidence.

3. Strategic Self-Promotion

Women are less likely to self-promote due to concerns over appearing boastful. Yet promoting one’s achievements strategically — framing them in terms of team or organizational success — enhances perceived credibility without triggering backlash.

4. Network-Driven Communication

Research in social network dynamics found that women in high-status roles not only need broad networks (for market information), but also tight-knit inner circles of other women (for gender-specific guidance). Strong communication within these circles helps women navigate unseen pitfalls and align their messaging with unwritten organizational norms. Engaging with networks like the Jacksonville Women’s Leadership Forum can help you develop and nurture those crucial relationships.

Evidence-Based Tips to Upgrade Your Communication

  • Assertive voice — prevents being overshadowed in meetings. Practice speaking early; state key points in the first 60 seconds; record and adjust tone in coaching sessions.
  • Transformational style — drives engagement and team performance. Start presentations with “Here’s what we achieved… and here’s why it matters to our team”; ask open-ended questions.
  • Self-promotion balance — builds visibility without backlash. Use “we” narratives: “Our team delivered a 20% increase… by implementing…”; cite team wins.
  • Network communication — unlocks hidden opportunities. Schedule monthly peer check-in calls; share insider updates and themes; offer mutual referrals.
  • Feedback and adaptation — keeps communication authentic and effective. After key meetings, ask trusted colleagues: “Did my tone come across as collaborative and confident? Where could I lean in more?”

Final Thoughts

Strong communication is more than a soft skill — it’s a strategic lever that shapes how organizations perceive you, what roles you’re offered, and how long you thrive in senior leadership. As women advance, refining communication style — asserting ideas, celebrating wins, building trusted networks, and seeking candid feedback — can differentiate between simply “being at the table” and truly influencing what’s on it.
